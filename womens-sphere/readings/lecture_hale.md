## Professor Hale's Lecture

*Background on the changing role of women in the 1800s. As you read, highlight two kinds of things:*

- ***terms you'll meet again in the readings** — like the Second Great Awakening, the "cult of domesticity," or the "public" and "domestic" spheres — and add a note saying what each one means in your own words;*
- ***the reasons Professor Hale gives for why these ideas about women arose when they did** — you'll need these at the end of the module, when you compare the 1800s ideal with today's.*

You may be familiar with the idea of a "tradwife" — the modern movement that promotes the idea that women are happier staying home, filling more traditional, stereotypical roles. This is not a new idea, though what you observe today is in some ways very different from what we will encounter in the texts we will look at. I want you to think about why.

We are talking about the "cult of domesticity" from the antebellum period — the time prior to the Civil War. It is important to understand a few things about this period. One was industrialization—the growth of railroads and major industries in the US. Another was a general sense among people in America that as the country developed, society could be improved. There was also, especially in the South, a desire to hold on to defined gender roles.

You will see a reference in your reading to the Second Great Awakening. This was a massive Christian religious revival that influenced many areas of society, including ideas about the family and the home.

In the antebellum period, middle- and upper-class women would not frequently have worked outside the home, whereas women from lower economic class groups would have done so out of necessity. So keep in mind, as you read, that the ideal of the "true woman" at home applied far more to some women than to others.
